How I came to Computer Science

My story starts when my father gave me his old work notebook, a Fujitsu. He mainly gave it to me so I could play Minecraft, but I quickly discovered modifications, or "mods". Installing mods on Java was tricky if you weren't familiar with computers, so I had to quickly learn how to navigate through my notebook and Windows 7.

I soon got the hang of it and tried to install a ton of mods, which failed due to hardware limitations. So I wished for a new notebook and got an HP Pavilion with Windows 10. I hated the transition at first. Nothing was where it used to be in Windows 7. But after adjusting, I jumped back into modding and started learning basic code. I wanted to understand how blocks, items, and mechanics came to life. Modding back then wasn't easy, so I eventually got tired of it, but I had already planted the seed: I knew basic Java and knew I wanted to code.

Learning to code sounds easier than it is, especially for a 14-year-old. I picked up where I left off with Java. When my father noticed my ambitions, he gave me an Arduino Uno. My first project was the classic "Blinking LED", which opened up a whole new world of possibilities. Back then, I didn't even realize I was writing C++ instead of Java, but at that age and framework, it didn't really matter.

For a few years, my coding journey came to a halt. I lost focus and got caught up in the school and gaming cycle with friends. But at the end of 8th grade, we got to choose an elective subject for 9th and 10th grade. While some students chose Latin (which my mother thankfully held me back from), I chose what I had pushed aside for years: Computer Science. My best friend and I enrolled together, heading to the top floor of the school every week to dive into CS.

Computer Science in my state didn't have a strict curriculum back then. For some students, that was a nightmare; for us, it was a blessing. While other classes learned how to format tables in Excel, we learned about handling electronic data, programming Android apps, and designing websites on CodePen. I was having the time of my life. Whenever a task was assigned, I finished early, added extra features, and spent the remaining time helping classmates with theirs. I had truly found my passion.

After finishing 10th grade and entering high school ("Abitur"), CS was no longer offered. I tried personal coding projects, though with mixed success. Still, my goal was set: after getting my Abitur, I wanted to study Computer Science at university. Fast forward to today: I'm currently in my 5th semester at Ruhr-Universität Bochum. (Spoiler alert: of the eight friends who started with me, only four of us remain!)

What's Next?

Studying Computer Science definitely has its challenging moments, but facing complex problems taught me adaptibility and qualities that grew naturally out of my passion for the field. At university, I didn't just learn how to write code; I built a deep understanding of computer science theory, essential mathematics, and the bridge between hardware and software. If you're interested, you can read my course summaries and takeaways here.

While my academic background gave me a solid foundation in Python, Java, and C, I am actively expanding my practical skill set through hands-on projects. Building this website is a great example: it serves as my testing ground for HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and TypeScript, with modern web development frameworks coming next.

My greatest strengths are my endless thirst for knowledge and a constant stream of new ideas. I've always had an analytical mindset, approaching challenges logically, recognizing patterns quickly, and finding structured solutions to complex problems.
